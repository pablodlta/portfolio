Koky: Engineering Case Study

Lead CAD & Packaging Engineer | Academic Project

1. Functional & Technical Constraints

The goal was to design an impact-resistant, ergonomic cosmetic container for pet care professionals and home users. Glass was ruled out to mitigate shattering risks around animals. Aluminium was selected for the primary container due to its chemical inertness with fragrance formulations, infinite recyclability, and low wall thickness capabilities.

2. CAD Surface Modeling & Parametric Tree

The geometric architecture was developed through precise procedural modeling:

  • Base body transitioned from a cylindrical footprint to a faceted gem cross-section using multi-plane guide curves (Loft/Recubrir).
  • Tactile grip area created by two opposed helical trajectories (clockwise and counter-clockwise sweeps) arrayed via circular body patterns and bounded by an extruded cut frame.
  • Cap designed via guide curves matching the bottle's hexagonal facets; animal paw identifiers embossed on dual faces using tangent sketch projections (Wrap/Envolver).
  • Threadless coupling achieved through calibrated friction grooves modeled via swept cuts across the mating parts.

4. Secondary Packaging & Palletization (ISO 3394)

The secondary packaging features a rigid hexagonal folding box (190 mm height, 78 mm width) with an interior molded pulp cushioning tray. Structured to pack inside 600x400 mm master cartons, achieving 21 units per layer on standard EPAL 1 pallets (1200x800 mm) across 4 stack tiers (84 units total) within transport height limits.

5. Physical Prototyping

Full-scale functional prototypes were printed using FDM additive manufacturing in PLA. Post-processed with metallic primers and selective enamel paints to evaluate hand clearances, single-handed spray ergonomics, and friction closure retention.
